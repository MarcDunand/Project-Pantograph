# What a pen plotter can and can't do

Pantograph only plots what is physically possible with one pen on paper, so an effect has to work within these limits:

- There is one pen, with one colour and one line width. An effect can't change colour, line width or pen, and it can't fill areas except by drawing lines (hatching).
- Ink can't be erased. An effect can never remove or move a mark that has already been plotted.
- Effects run live, one command at a time, as the stroke arrives. An effect can change the stroke in progress (reshape it, drop points, hold points back until the stroke ends), and it can add marks of its own, usually right after a stroke ends (see after_stroke). It can remember every earlier stroke, but it can't know about future strokes.
- The effect doesn't know the iPad's brush, colour or line width, only positions and pressure.
- Positions are in inches on the machine, measured from its home corner: x along its long axis, from 0 to ctx.x_max; y along its short axis, from 0 to ctx.y_max (about 11.8 × 8.6 for a standard AxiDraw). Pantograph clamps everything to that area. The drawing can be placed and turned on the paper freely, so don't rely on which way is "up" or "left". In the usual layout, the drawing's left–right runs along y and its top–bottom along x.
- Incoming points are about 0.25 mm (0.01 in) apart. Don't make segments much shorter than that: the plotter can't draw them any better.
- Pressure is 0 to 1 (0.5 when the iPad sends none). It changes the pen's height only if the person has switched on "vary with pencil pressure"; otherwise it is just data the effect can use.
- The plotter is slower than a hand. Every pen lift costs a moment, and long extra lines take time. Keep what the effect adds in proportion to the stroke, or the plotter falls far behind the artist.
- Effects must be quick: Pantograph waits on every call, and switches off an effect that takes more than a second for one point.

If what was asked for can't be done on a pen plotter, write the closest thing that can, and say so plainly in the blurb.
